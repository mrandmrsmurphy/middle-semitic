| Notes | The creeping of many legs and limbs, and of teeming swarms: worms, insects, reptiles, the small crowding life of the ground and water (Gen 1:28 "every living thing that creeps"). Hebrew רָמַשׂ is "creep, teem; move lightly, glide about (of water animals)" (BDB; Klein: "creep, crawl, swarm; teem, said of the earth"), with the noun רֶמֶשׂ "creeping things". The Hebrew *ś* gives MS ܫ by the chart on [[Anthropology]]. No cognate verb is found elsewhere: Aramaic רמש means "evening" (Jastrow) and the Targum and Peshitta use רחש / ܪܚܫ for this sense; Arabic رَمَشَ "grab with the fingertips; blink" is of unknown origin and unrelated. Branch vote: NW (Hebrew) R-M-Ś, (Aramaic) R-Ḥ-Š; Arabic D-B-B / Z-Ḥ-F; ruling by the sense split: R-M-Š takes the swarming creep, [[Lexicon/ܕܒܒ\|ܕܒܒ]] the single creature's creep and stealth. The noun is the archaism [[Lexicon/ܪܡܫܐ\|ܪܡܫܐ]]. Akkadian, Ugaritic and Ge'ez not checked. |
