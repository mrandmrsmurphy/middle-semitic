| Notes | Derived from PS *ḏrḥ by the consonant chart on [[Anthropology]]: *ḏ* is MS z ܙ (Hebrew merged it into ז, Aramaic into ד), *r* is r ܪ, *ḥ* is ḥ ܚ. Branch vote: NW (Hebrew) Ḏ-R-Ḥ "rise, shine"; Arabic has no reflex of the root (a regular ذَرَحَ is absent), and its word for the concept, شَرَقَ "to rise (of the sun), to shine" — whence شَرْق and مَشْرِق "east", the same sunrise → east pathway as Hebrew מִזְרָח — is a different root (ش is PS *ś and ق is PS *q, whose regular Hebrew reflexes are שׁ and ק, as in שָׁרַק "to hiss, whistle"), a semantic counterpart, not a cognate; outgroups Akkadian zarâḫu "to rise" (cited by BDB from Delitzsch, not independently verified) and Sabaean ḏrḥ "lofty, majestic" (BDB) support *ḏ; ruling by the chart and the outgroups. Hebrew uses זָרַח of the sun in every period (BDB: Gen 32:32, Ps 104:22, Eccl 1:5; Jastrow: Ḥull. 91b; modern זוֹרַחַת); its מִזְרָח "east" is the model for [[Lexicon/ܡܙܪܚܐ\|ܡܙܪܚܐ]]. Aramaic ܕܢܰܚ "to shine, be bright" (Jastrow; Targ. Gen 32:32) has the regular *ḏ* → d but an unexplained r → n. Ugaritic not checked. |
