| Notes | Derived in-language from MS [[Lexicon/ܣܚܢ\|ܣܚܢ]] *saḥan* "to heat" on the same root. Aramaic ܫܚܝܢܐ is exactly this word (š where MS has s); Arabic سَاخِن is the active participle "hot" of *saḵuna* "be hot" (the dialects' word too — Egyptian صخن); the Hebrew trace is שְׁחִין "boil" (the Exodus plague), the hot root drifted to the sore. Not coined from Ḥ-M-M (Hebrew חַם "hot", Eblaite *ḥammūtum*): that root stays with the in-laws [[Lexicon/ܚܡܐ\|ܚܡܐ]], and remains the natural future word for "hot". Ugaritic not checked. |

## Forms

Adjective: it agrees with its noun in gender and number, and has the four forms below (masculine *-â*, feminine *-tâ*, plural *-în* and *-ût*). The construct forms are frozen and an archaism. A crypto-feminine noun takes the feminine forms. See [[Morphophonology]].
