| Notes | The elevated word for speech: a discourse, a debate, a disputation, a speech that matters (the "words" of Ex 20:1, where God addresses the people). It is not the small, plain "word", which is [[Lexicon/ܐܡܪܬܐ\|ܐܡܪܬܐ]]. Arabic كَلَام is "speech, talk, conversation, language; discussion, debate, dispute; words, saying", and in Islam *kalām* is the discipline of theological debate; كَلِمَة "word, statement" and the verb كَلَّمَ (form II) "speak to, address" are the same root, as is the Arabic Bible's كَلِمَات in Ex 20:1 (Smith–Van Dyck). The root's older sense is "bite, wound" (Arabic كَلْم; Wiktionary compares Akkadian *kalmatu* "insect"), so speaking is a later development; Hebrew and Aramaic have no cognate noun for speech (the Hebrew and Aramaic words for "word" are on other roots). The form follows the regular noun template (*kalmâ*, like [[Lexicon/ܐܡܪܐ\|ܐܡܪܐ]]), not the Arabic long vowel. Branch vote: Arabic only (K-L-M); no NW or outgroup word for speech; ruling by the user's sense split: K-L-M is the noble, formal speech; A-M-R the plain word and the plain verb. Ugaritic, Akkadian and Ge'ez not checked for this sense. |
