| Notes | An archaism, not an everyday word: the biblical and literary name for the swarming, many-legged life of the ground and water, the "creeping things" (Hebrew רֶמֶשׂ, BDB: "creeping things, moving things; gliding things of sea animals"). It is a collective, used mostly in the singular for the whole class. The ordinary words are the specific names ([[Lexicon/ܬܘܠܥܬܐ\|ܬܘܠܥܬܐ]] "worm", [[Lexicon/ܕܘܕ\|ܕܘܕ]] "worms", [[Lexicon/ܢܚܣܐ\|ܢܚܣܐ]] "snake"). The verb is [[Lexicon/ܪܡܫ\|ܪܡܫ]]; no cognate noun is found outside Hebrew. |

## Forms

Masculine declension. There is no dual: the dual is frozen and only body parts, glasses and pants have one. The construct state is frozen and an archaism. See [[Morphophonology]]. The forms are built on the stem in the Latin row above.
