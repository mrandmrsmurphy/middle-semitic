| Notes | The small, plain word: a word, a saying, a remark, something someone said, with nothing grand about it. Hebrew אִמְרָה is "word, speech, saying" (Klein) and Neo-Aramaic ܐܡܪܬܐ *immārtā* is "saying, sentence, statement" (Wiktionary, as the synonym of *milṯā*); Arabic has no noun of this form (أَمْر is "command", on [[Lexicon/ܐܡܪܐ\|ܐܡܪܐ]]). It is the noun of the plain verb [[Lexicon/ܐܡܪ\|ܐܡܪ]]. The elevated speech, the discourse or debate, is [[Lexicon/ܟܠܡܐ\|ܟܠܡܐ]]. The spelling ܐܡܪܬܐ is also the 2m past of ܐܡܪ (*âmartâ* "you said"), a homograph the context separates. |

## Forms

True feminine declension. There is no dual: the dual is frozen and only body parts, glasses and pants have one. The construct state is frozen and an archaism. See [[Morphophonology]]. The forms are built on the stem in the Latin row above.
