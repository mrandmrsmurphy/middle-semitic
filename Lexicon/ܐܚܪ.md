| Notes | Behind, in back of (place). Hebrew אַחַר is a noun "hind part" (Wiktionary: pattern *qeṭel*) used as a preposition "behind, after" (BDB, Klein), and Ugaritic *aḫr* is "after, behind" (from memory). The root is the same as [[Lexicon/ܐܚܪܐ|ܐܚܪܐ]] *ʾaḥêrâ* "other" (Arabic آخَر, Hebrew אַחֵר), and Arabic keeps the family (*ʾakhkhara* "delay, hold back", *ʾākhir* "last", *ʾukhur* "back part") but has no preposition from it: its "after" is *baʿda*. Sense split: ʾ-Ḥ-R carries "behind" (place) and B-ʿ-D, [[Lexicon/ܒܥܕ|ܒܥܕ]] *baʿad*, carries "after" (time, sequence). Branch vote: NW ʾ-Ḥ-R (Hebrew, Ugaritic; Aramaic has the verb "delay"); Arabic has the root without the preposition, and Arabic B-ʿ-D "after" gets the other sense. Outgroups not checked. A long preposition: free word with its own stress, suffixes straight onto the lexical form (*ʾa.ˈḥar.nî*), like [[Lexicon/ܬܚܬ|ܬܚܬ]] *taḥat*; the *ḥ* is written Ḥ, from PS *ḥ and *ḫ alike on the chart.
