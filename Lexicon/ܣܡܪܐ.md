| Notes | The colour comes from S-M-R, the Arabic root of أَسْمَر "brown, tawny; dark-skinned" (the Arabic verb سَمِرَ is "be or become brown"). Hebrew סָמַר is the same root with a different sense, "bristle up" (BDB, Klein; Modern Hebrew also "nail", as Arabic مِسْمَار "nail"); Hebrew has no colour word from it, and its "brown" חוּם is [[Lexicon/ܚܘܡܐ|ܚܘܡܐ]] "dark" here. Neo-Aramaic ܣܡܪܐ "tan, suntan" (same root); the adjective "brown" was not found. |

## Forms

Adjective: it agrees with its noun in gender and number, and has the four forms below (masculine *-â*, feminine *-tâ*, plural *-în* and *-ût*). The construct forms are frozen and an archaism. A crypto-feminine noun takes the feminine forms. See [[Morphophonology]].
