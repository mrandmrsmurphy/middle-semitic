| Notes | Across, beyond, through. Arabic عَبْرَ *ʿabra* "across" is the adverbial accusative of the noun عَبْر *ʿabr* (verbal noun of *ʿabara* "cross"); Hebrew עֵבֶר *ʿēver* "the region across or beyond, the other side" (BDB) is the noun, used after a preposition (*bĕʿēver* "across", *mēʿēver* "from beyond"); Aramaic *ʿbar* is the preposition "across, past; through, via" (Neo-Aramaic, from the construct of *ʿibrā* "far bank"); Ugaritic *ʿbr* and Akkadian *ebēru* have the verb. Branch vote: ʿ-B-R on every branch (Hebrew, Aramaic, Ugaritic, Arabic; Akkadian outgroup); the one root carries all three senses, so no second word is coined. "Beyond" is *min ʿabar* "from across", as Hebrew *mēʿēver*. Not used: Arabic وَرَاءَ *warāʾa* (W-R-ʾ "behind, beyond", Arabic only; MS has [[Lexicon/ܐܚܪ|ܐܚܪ]] *ʾaḥar* "behind") and Arabic خِلَالَ *ḫilāla* (Ḫ-L-L, "during, in the course of"; left for a future "during"). A long preposition: a free word with its own stress, suffixes straight onto the lexical form (*ʿa.ˈbar.nî*), like [[Lexicon/ܬܚܬ|ܬܚܬ]] *taḥat*, with an epenthetic *a* in the Latin row to keep the syllables CV.CVC; it is a homograph and homophone of the verb [[Lexicon/ܥܒܪ|ܥܒܪ]] *ʿavar* "to cross" (the begadkefat *b* is free between stop and fricative). Proto-Semitic *\*ʕabar-* (the verb's) |
