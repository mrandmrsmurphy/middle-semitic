| Notes | Until, up to, as far as. Hebrew עַד "as far as, even to, until, up to" (BDB), Aramaic עַד and Syriac ܥܕ *ʿaḏ* "until, up to, as far as, during" (Syriac also ܥܕܡܐ *ʿaḏmā*, *ʿaḏ* + *mā*), Ugaritic *ʿd*, Akkadian *adi*; Arabic has the dialectal variant عَتَّى *ʿattā* beside the standard حَتَّى *ḥattā* (Wiktionary). Probably related to the verb "pass on, advance" (Hebrew עָדָה, Arabic *ʿadā*), not verified. Branch vote: NW (Hebrew, Aramaic, Ugaritic) ʿ-D; Arabic ʿ-D in the variant *ʿattā*; outgroup Akkadian *adi*; ruling by the root. The competing words are not used: Arabic حَتَّى *ḥattā* (Arabic only, disputed origin, never takes suffixes, also "even") and إِلَى *ʾilā* ("to, toward" in every branch; "until" only in Arabic; MS *li-* covers "to"). A long preposition: a free word with its own stress, suffixes straight onto the lexical form (*ʿad.nî*), like [[Lexicon/ܬܚܬ|ܬܚܬ]] *taḥat*. No matching noun yet; with [[Lexicon/ܐܢ|ܐܢ]] *an* it introduces a finite “until” clause. With a negated main clause it can express a prerequisite (“unless”). See [[Morphosyntax#Subordinate clauses]]. Sabaean *ʿd* from memory, Ge'ez not checked |
