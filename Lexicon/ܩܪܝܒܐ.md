| Notes | Derived in-language from MS [[Lexicon/ܩܪܒ\|ܩܪܒ]] *qarab* "to come near" on the same root. The word is the same in all three sources and in both vernaculars: Hebrew קָרוֹב (the *qatîl* pattern with Hebrew's ô for î), Arabic قَرِيب, Syriac ܩܪܝܒܐ, Egyptian and Palestinian قريب. In substantive use, *qarîbâ* means “fellow,” as in possessed *qarîbâkâ* “your fellow” in [[Tanakh#Leviticus 19:18]]. This follows the Arabic verse and has a parallel in Aramaic “other, neighbor, companion.” Ugaritic not checked. [Aramaic](https://cal.huc.edu/getlex.php?coord=620300312&word=14) |

## Forms

Adjective: it agrees with its noun in gender and number, and has the four forms below (masculine *-â*, feminine *-tâ*, plural *-în* and *-ût*). The construct forms are frozen and an archaism. A crypto-feminine noun takes the feminine forms. See [[Morphophonology]].
