| Notes | Hebrew מָחָר "tomorrow, in time to come" in every period (BDB; modern מחר), Aramaic מְחַר (Targ. Ex 17:9, Prov 27:1) and Syriac ܡܚܰܪ (BDB). The etymology is disputed: BDB takes it as "time in front", comparing Akkadian maḫru "front" (of time, but of former time — the direction flipped); Jastrow derives it from אָחַר "to be late", "the following (day)". Either way the formation is transparent through the ʾ-Ḥ-R family (cf. [[Lexicon/ܐܚܪ\|ܐܚܪ]] "behind", [[Lexicon/ܒܥܕ\|ܒܥܕ]] "after", [[Lexicon/ܐܚܪܐ\|ܐܚܪܐ]] "other"). Arabic has no cognate, and its branch is split: MSA غَدًا and Moroccan غدا (from غ-د-ا "early morning") against Egyptian/Levantine/Hijazi بكرة (from ب-ك-ر "to be early"); ruled by NW unity. Akkadian maḫru via BDB. Used as a bare time word, as in Hebrew; "today" is compositional (ܗܠ ܝܘܡܐ), and "the day after tomorrow" can follow Aramaic מחרא דמחר and Arabic بعد بكرة with *maḥar* and [[Lexicon/ܒܥܕ\|ܒܥܕ]]. |
