| Notes | Hebrew חוּם is "dark brown, black" (BDB: only of the colour of sheep, Gen 30:32-40; Klein: "dark brown, black"; Modern Hebrew "brown"), the cognate of the root. Middle Semitic takes the root for "dark" in general and uses [[Lexicon/ܣܡܪܐ|ܣܡܪܐ]] for "brown". The hollow root contracts *aw* to *ô* before a consonant (*ḥawamâ* becomes *ḥômâ*), as in the verbs; this is the same rule, not yet ruled for adjectives. |

## Forms

Adjective: it agrees with its noun in gender and number, and has the four forms below (masculine *-â*, feminine *-tâ*, plural *-în* and *-ût*). The construct forms are frozen and an archaism. A crypto-feminine noun takes the feminine forms. See [[Morphophonology]].
