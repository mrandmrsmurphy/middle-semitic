| Notes | The name that stands for יהוה. Jewish tradition does not pronounce the Tetragrammaton and reads *Adonai* ("my lord", the plural of majesty with the suffix of the first person) in its place, so MS does the same: nobody is asked to attempt the unspoken Name. Hebrew אָדוֹן is "lord, master" (Wiktionary: Proto-West Semitic *ʔadān-, perhaps from a Proto-Semitic *ʔadan-* "judge, rule"). The ordinary word for a human lord is not this: it is [[Lexicon/ܪܒܐ\|ܪܒܐ]]. Names do not inflect: no plural, construct or suffixed forms, so the final *-î* is part of the name and is not the possessive suffix. Arabic and Aramaic have no cognate used here (the Arabic Bible has *ar-rabb* and the Peshitta ܡܪܝܐ, other words). Ugaritic and Akkadian not checked. |
