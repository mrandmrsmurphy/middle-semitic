| Notes | Physical hardness, as of stone or a surface. The [[Anthropology]] chart maps earlier *š* (Hebrew and Syriac š, Arabic s) to ܣ. The adjective follows Syriac *qašyā* in shape and takes the Northwest Semitic final yod. Arabic قَاسٍ belongs to the root Q-S-W and is attested of hard stone. Branch vote: NW (Hebrew and Aramaic) Q-Š-Y “hard”; Arabic Q-S-W “hard, firm,” alongside Ṣ-L-B “solid, rigid”; ruling by shared physical sense and the Northwest Semitic adjective form. ## Forms

The stem keeps *y* before vowel-initial adjective endings. The feminine singular has *î* before *-tâ* by analogy with the weak noun stems in [[Morphophonology]].
