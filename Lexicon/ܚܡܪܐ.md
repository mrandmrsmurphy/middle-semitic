| Notes | The colour comes from Ḥ-M-R, the Arabic root of أَحْمَر. Hebrew חָמַר "be red" is a rare verb (Klein; BDB refers to it as IV חמר, Job 16:16 חֳמַרְמְרָה), and the Hebrew everyday word is אָדֹם. Aramaic ܣܘܡܩܐ is a functional match from a different root; Aramaic ܚܡܪܐ is "wine", and Hebrew חֲמוֹר "donkey" (the root's other word, [[Lexicon/ܚܡܘܪܐ\|ܚܡܘܪܐ]]) is explained by BDB as "named from its reddish colour". |

## Forms

Adjective: it agrees with its noun in gender and number, and has the four forms below (masculine *-â*, feminine *-tâ*, plural *-în* and *-ût*). The construct forms are frozen and an archaism. A crypto-feminine noun takes the feminine forms. See [[Morphophonology]].
