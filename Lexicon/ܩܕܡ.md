| Notes | Before, in front of, in the presence of: the preposition of Exodus 20:3 ("no other gods before me"). The root Q-D-M "precede, come before" (Wiktionary: PS *ḳ-d-m) is in all three languages: Hebrew קֶדֶם "front, that which is before; east; antiquity" and Biblical Hebrew קֹדֶם / מִקֶּדֶם, Aramaic קֳדָם *qŏḏām*, the standard Aramaic preposition "before, in front of" (BDB; Syriac ܩܕܡ), Ugaritic *qdm* "before, in front of" (UgaritLab, Wiktionary), and Arabic قَدَمَ "precede" and قُدَّامَ *quddāma* "before, in front of" (Wiktionary: an Aramaic loan, beside the native أَمَامَ *amāma*, from ʾ-M-M, and the Hebrew idiom עַל־פָּנָי "upon my face", from P-N-Y). Branch vote: NW (Hebrew, Aramaic, Ugaritic) Q-D-M; Arabic Q-D-M (verb and the loan) beside ʾ-M-M; ruling by the widest root, as the one root shared by all three languages. A long preposition: a free word with its own stress, not a clitic, and it takes the pronominal suffixes straight onto the lexical form (*qa.ˈdam.nî*), like *min*, *ʿal-*, *ʿim* and *bên*; the epenthetic *a* breaks the cluster *dm*, so the Latin row has two syllables (*qa.ˈdam*, final closed syllable stressed). Akkadian and Ge'ez not checked. |
