Arabic has plain t (ت) where the chart predicts ṭ (ط); this is the one irregular source. Hebrew קָטַל is rare in the Bible (Psalm 139:19, Job 13:15 and 24:14, per Klein; the noun קֶטֶל in Obadiah 9) and Hebrew's everyday verb is הָרַג, but the root is the common Aramaic word for "kill" and the Hebrew grammar-book model verb. Ugaritic qṭl, Akkadian *qatālu* and Ge'ez *qaṭala* recalled from memory, unverified. |

### Definitions

- **G** *qaṭal* ܩܛܠ: to kill.
- **Gp** *qûṭal* ܩܘܛܠ: passive of G: to be killed.
- **D** *qîṭṭal* ܩܝܛܠ: to massacre, to slaughter.
- **Dp** *qûṭṭal* ܩܘܛܠ: passive of D: to be massacred.
- **tD** *tiqîṭṭal* ܬܩܝܛܠ: to kill one another, to fight to the death.
- This verb has no C, Cp stems.

### Recognizing the stems

In the unvocalized spelling (the gemination mark is optional and left out), the past 3m of each stem is: G ܩܛܠ; Gp ܩܘܛܠ; D ܩܝܛܠ; Dp ܩܘܛܠ; tD ܬܩܝܛܠ. Gp and Dp are spelled alike unless the shadda is written, as in Hebrew and Arabic.
