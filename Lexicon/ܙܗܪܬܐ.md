| Notes | Arabic زَهْرَة is the singulative of زَهْر "flowers; splendor": the root is "to shine, be radiant" and the flower is the shining thing (compare زُهْرَة "brilliance; the planet Venus"). Hebrew זֹהַר "brightness, splendor" — the word of the Zohar — is the same root with the unspecialized sense. The dialects keep زهرة (also "cauliflower" in Gulf and Levantine); Spanish *azahar* "orange blossom" is a loan from Arabic. A Syriac verb ܙܗܰܪ "to shine" is recalled but unchecked. Ugaritic and Proto-Semitic not checked. |

## Forms

True feminine declension. There is no dual: the dual is frozen and only body parts, glasses and pants have one. The construct state is frozen and an archaism. See [[Morphophonology]].
