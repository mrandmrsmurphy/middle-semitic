| Notes | The plain, everyday "he said" of dialogue narration, with the quote after it (*âmar lahû: …*). Derived from PS *ʾmr* by the consonant chart on [[Anthropology]]: all three radicals are regular, and Hebrew, Aramaic and Ugaritic agree. Branch vote: NW (Hebrew, Aramaic, Ugaritic) ʾ-M-R; Arabic Q-W-L; outgroup neither as a verb for "say"; ruling by the breadth of the root (Ge'ez *ʾamara* "show, declare" per BDB; Akkadian *amārum* "see" per Wiktionary) and Hebrew's weight. The Arabic cognate أَمَرَ means only "to command", which is Hebrew's second sense too; MS keeps "command" for the noun [[Lexicon/ܐܡܪܐ\|ܐܡܪܐ]], and Arabic's everyday قَالَ goes to the root [[Roots/ܩ-ܘ-ܠ\|ܩ-ܘ-ܠ]], which means to sound forth, a generic word for the voice (including animals'). BDB suggests the root's original sense was "be or make prominent" (Sabaean *ʾmr* "lofty"). Hebrew אָמַר also covers "he thought, said in his heart". |

### Definitions

- **G** *âmar* ܐܡܪ: to say.
- **Gp** *ʾûmar* ܐܘܡܪ: passive of G: to be said.
- **C** *šâmar* ܫܐܡܪ: to make say, to have (someone) say.
- **Cp** *šûʾmar* ܫܘܐܡܪ: passive of C: to be made to say.
- This verb has no D, Dp, tD stems.

### Recognizing the stems

In the unvocalized spelling (the gemination mark is optional and left out), the past 3m of each stem is: G ܐܡܪ; Gp ܐܘܡܪ; C ܫܐܡܪ; Cp ܫܘܐܡܪ.
