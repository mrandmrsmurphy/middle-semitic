## Forms

Masculine declension. The construct state is frozen and an archaism, but it is the natural form on these words because of the many ancient texts (compare Hebrew *avinu malkeinu*, 'our father, our king'). The dual is frozen and only body parts, glasses and pants have one, so 'two kings' is the plural. See [[Morphophonology]].

| | singular | plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| regular | ܡܰܠܟܰܐ *malkâ* ܡܠܟܐ | ܡܰܠܟܽܝܢ *malkîn* ܡܠܟܝܢ |
| *construct* | ܡܰܠܱܟ *malak* ܡܠܟ | ܡܰܠܟܺܝ *malkî* ܡܠܟܝ |

### With pronominal suffixes

The pronominal suffixes attach to the construct state, with nothing dropped: singular *malak* + suffix, plural *malkî* + suffix. Where the stem ends in the same consonant as the suffix begins, the result is a doubled letter (written with *shadda* in vocalized text), which marks the suffix.
