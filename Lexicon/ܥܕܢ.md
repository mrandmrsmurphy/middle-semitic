| Notes | From Aramaic ܥܻܕܳܢ *ʿiddān* "time" (Biblical Aramaic, Dan; Jastrow). The Hebrew sister form is עֵת "time" — BDB derives it from a root ענה (Phoenician עת; Talmudic עַנְתָּא), so the exact root shape is debated and the reconstruction is marked hypothetical; Akkadian *inu* / *ittu* "time" is the outgroup (via BDB). Arabic has no cognate: its "time" is وَقْت or زَمَن, from unrelated roots. "Now" is the transparent phrase ܥܕܢ ܗܙܐ *ʿidân hazâ* "this time" — the formation every branch froze into a word: Hebrew עַתָּה (accusative of עֵת "at the time", BDB), Aramaic כְּעַן (contraction of כְּעִידָּן "at the time", Jastrow), Arabic الْآن ("at the time", BDB) and dialectal دلوقتي "this-time", هسه "this-hour". Masculine, with Aramaic and Arabic; Hebrew עֵת is feminine. |
