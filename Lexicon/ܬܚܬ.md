| Notes | Under, beneath: the preposition of the noun [[Lexicon/ܬܚܬܐ\|ܬܚܬܐ]] "the underside, the bottom". In all three languages the preposition is the noun used as a place word: Hebrew תַּחַת "the under part; beneath, under; instead of" (BDB, Klein), Arabic تَحْتَ *taḥta* (the adverbial accusative of the noun تَحْت "the place below", also *min taḥti* "from below"), Syriac ܬܚܬ, Ugaritic *tḥt* /taḥta/ "under", and Ge'ez *taḥt* (Wiktionary; the Ge'ez is from there too). Branch vote: NW (Hebrew, Aramaic, Ugaritic) T-Ḥ-T; Arabic T-Ḥ-T; outgroup Ge'ez T-Ḥ-T; ruling by the root every branch shares. The competing words are not used for "under": Arabic أَسْفَلَ *asfala* (S-F-L, "low") and دُونَ *dūna* (D-N-W, "below, less than, without"), Hebrew לְמַטָּה "down, below" (M-Ṭ-Y). A long preposition: a free word with its own stress, not a clitic, taking the pronominal suffixes straight onto the lexical form (*ta.ˈḥat.nî*), like [[Lexicon/ܩܕܡ\|ܩܕܡ]] *qadam*; the written *-ḥt* is already the Hebrew and Arabic pronunciation, with an epenthetic *a* in the Latin row to keep the syllables CV.CVC. Akkadian not checked. |
