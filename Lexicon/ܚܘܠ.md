| Notes | Around, round about. Arabic حَوْلَ *ḥawla* "around, surrounding, concerning" is the adverbial accusative of the noun حَوْل *ḥawl* "surroundings" (verb حَالَ *ḥāla* "turn, change, intervene"); Hebrew has the verb חוּל *ḥûl* "whirl, twist, dance" (BDB; the name *Hul* is glossed "circle") and Aramaic *ḥûl* "turn around, circle" (Jastrow), without a prepositional use. Branch vote: Arabic Ḥ-W-L as the preposition and Hebrew and Aramaic Ḥ-W-L in the same turning sense; the NW word סָבִיב *savîv* (S-B-B, Hebrew and Ugaritic only), Aramaic ܚܕܪ *ḥḏar* (Ḥ-D-R, Aramaic only) and D-W-R (a verb and noun only) are not used. The root also holds [[Lexicon/ܚܘܠܐ|ܚܘܠܐ]] *ḥôlâ* "sand"; the link between the senses is not established. A long preposition: a free word with its own stress, suffixes straight onto the lexical form (*ḥa.ˈwal.nî*), like [[Lexicon/ܬܚܬ|ܬܚܬ]] *taḥat*, with an epenthetic *a* in the Latin row to keep the syllables CV.CVC. No matching noun: the regular form of "surroundings" would be spelled ܚܘܠܐ, the same as the sand word. Ugaritic and the outgroups not checked |
