| Notes | The word of a speaker with authority: a command, an order, a decree. Arabic أَمْر "command, order" (also "matter, affair") is the verbal noun of أَمَرَ; Hebrew אֹמֶר (BDB: "utterance, speech, word, saying, promise, command") and the feminine אִמְרָה "word, speech, saying" (Klein) are the Hebrew nouns. The ordinary word for what someone said is not this but the plain verb [[Lexicon/ܐܡܪ\|ܐܡܪ]] and its small noun [[Lexicon/ܐܡܪܬܐ\|ܐܡܪܬܐ]] "word, saying"; the voice itself is [[Lexicon/ܩܘܠܐ\|ܩܘܠܐ]]. Ugaritic *amr* "tidings, message" (UgaritLab, German *Kunde*) is recalled but its relation unverified. Syriac ܐܡܪܐ is a different word, "lamb" (from Akkadian *immeru*); MS spells the two alike but they are unrelated. Aramaic not checked. |

## Forms

Masculine declension. There is no dual: the dual is frozen and only body parts, glasses and pants have one. The construct state is frozen and an archaism. See [[Morphophonology]]. The forms are built on the stem in the Latin row above.
