| Notes | Arabic وَرَقَة is the general word for "leaf" (collective وَرَق), also "sheet of paper" and "banknote". Biblical Hebrew יָרָק means "greens, vegetables" — the leaf sense shifted to produce. Akkadian *warqum* is a cognate (Wiktionary). The root already gives MS its word for "green", [[Lexicon/ܘܪܩܐ\|ܘܪܩܐ]]: leaf and green are one Proto-Semitic family, so the noun's forms are homophonous with the feminine forms of that adjective. The Aramaic ܘܪܩܬܐ is a Neo-Aramaic loan from Arabic; native Aramaic "leaf" is ܛܰܪܦܳܐ, a different root. |

## Forms

True feminine declension. There is no dual: the dual is frozen and only body parts, glasses and pants have one. The construct state is frozen and an archaism. See [[Morphophonology]].
