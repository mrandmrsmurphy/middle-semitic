# Prepositions

> Parent: [[Middle Semitic]] · See also: [[Morphosyntax]] (the conjugation table and subordinate clauses)

Middle Semitic has eight "small" prepositions, which take pronominal suffixes and so "conjugate" (*ʿalnî* 'upon me'). Every other "preposition" is really a noun in the construct state. Each inflected form is a lexicon entry of its own; the full paradigms are in [[Morphosyntax]].

*min* and *ʿal-* also have clitic forms, [[Lexicon/ܡ|ܡ-]] *mi-* and [[Lexicon/ܥ|ܥ-]] *ʿa-*, each with suffixed forms of its own (*minî*, *mikâ*...; *ʿanî*, *ʿakâ*...; the full words' are *minnî*, *minkâ*... and *ʿalnî*, *ʿalkâ*...). The clitics (*li- bi- pi- ki- mi- ʿa-*) are single consonant + vowel: they attach to the following word, take no stress, and may be said with a schwa in fast speech (*ləMadrid*). The other forms (*min*, *ʿal-*, *ʿim*, *bên*) are full words with their own stress. 

## Contrasts

**Location: *pi-* vs *bi-* vs *ʿal-*.** *pi-* is containment ('in Madrid', 'in the house'); *bi-* is contact or point location ('at the door', 'on the table'); *ʿal-* is 'on, upon', with the sense of being over or resting on. *bi-* and *ʿal-* overlap on 'on'. *bi-* is the near, neutral 'on/at'; *ʿal-* stresses support or superposition.

**Accompaniment and likeness: *ʿim* vs *ki-* vs *bên*.** *ʿim* is accompaniment ('with him'); *ki-* is comparison ('like him'): the same person, but together versus resembling. *bên* is 'between', location relative to two or more.

**Source vs goal: *min* vs *li-*.** *min* is the source or starting point ('from Madrid'); *li-* is the goal, recipient or beneficiary ('to Madrid', 'for her'). They are the two ends of motion and transfer.

**Dative and benefactive: *li-*.** *li-* carries the person who receives or benefits: *linî* 'for me, to me'. It is also the first half of a reason connective (see below).

## Distinguishing homographs

The suffixed form of *bi-* is ܒܢܝ 2 *binî* 'on me', a homograph of the verb ܒܢܝ *banê* 'he built'. The other suffixed forms are unambiguous.

## Not prepositions

Several words that English glosses with a preposition are not among the eight:

- [[Lexicon/ܐܝܠܐ|ܐܝܠܐ]] *ʾêllâ* 'except, excluding' (*if* + *not*) is a fixed compound that behaves as a preposition; [[Lexicon/ܐܝܠܠܐ|ܐܝܠܠܐ]] *ella* 'except, but' is the conjunction.
- Subordinators: [[Lexicon/ܐܢ|ܐܢ]] *ʾan* 'that' (complementizer), [[Lexicon/ܟܝ|ܟܝ]] *kî* 'in order that', [[Lexicon/ܐܡ|ܐܡ]] *ʾam* 'if', and the relative pronoun [[Lexicon/ܠܝܕ|ܠܝܕ]] *lîd*. These join clauses and do not take suffixes.
- Spatial and temporal relations beyond the eight ('before', 'after', 'under', 'until', 'around', 'without') are expressed with nouns in the construct state, as in the rest of Semitic; none have been coined yet.

## Open

- Extended uses of the eight (possession with *li-*, comparison with *min*, instrument with *bi-*) are not yet ruled.
