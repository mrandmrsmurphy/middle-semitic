# Orthography

> Parent: [[Middle Semitic]] · Sounds: [[Phonology]] · Rationale: [[Anthropology#Orthography]]

*Rationale: the script is Syriac, the classical written form of Aramaic and the existing common ground between Hebrew and Arabic; choosing either one's alphabet would upset their equal footing. Latin would signal the West and colonial influence, so it appears only as a transliteration aid. An abjad shows the triconsonantal root, writing runs right to left as in both source scripts, and everyone learns the same small alphabet, so the cost is equal and many shapes are already familiar. Vowel marks and the begadkefat dots are optional, as pointing is in the sources. See [[Design Philosophy#Syriac as the script]].*

Syriac script, written **RTL**. Equal distance from Arabic and Israeli — Neo-Aramaic as the middle ground.

Letter names: ʾalap, bet, gamal, dalat, he, wo, zen, ḥe, ṭet, yod, kap, lamad, mim, nun, samkat, ʿe, pe, ṣade, qop, reš, šin, to.

For purposes of weak conjugations/declensions, all pharyngeals and glottals are considered “gutturals”. For the “begadkepat” letters, they are in free variation between stops and fricatives. However, most people say them as fricatives intervocalically and as stops elsewhere. (When they are doubled, they are always stops.) However, in spelling certain foreign words and in full-spelling, it is possible to distinguish between the sounds by a dot above for the stop, and a dot below for the fricative.

- The dots: ܒ̇ is /b/ while ܒ̣ is /v/
- The dots: ܓ̇ is /g/ while ܓ̣ is /ɣ/
- The dots: ܕ̇ is /d/ while ܕ̣ is /ð/
- The dots: ܟ̇ is /k/ while ܟ̣ is /x/
- The dots: ܦ̇ is /p/ while ܦ̣ is /f/
- The dots: ܬ̇ is /t/ while ܬ̣ is /θ/

Gemination uses Arabic *shadda*. Glottal stop as a consonant (vs quiescent / *mater* alif) is marked with a superscript alif, as in Aramaic.

Keyboard hints (common Syriac layouts): *ḥbaṣā* i (shift-F below, shift-R above); *ʾṣāṣā* u (shift-G, shift-T); *rbāṣā* e (shift-E, shift-D); *zqāp̄ā* o (shift-S, shift-W); *pṯāḥā* a (shift-A, shift-Q).

### Diacritics

*a*, *w*, and *y* are assumed to always be *mater lectionis*, so when they are consonants (in full spelling), they get a superscript alef, which looks like this: ܐܑ ܁ ܘܑ ܁ ܝܑ (shift-H on common layouts).
