The paradigm pass finished 2026-10-28; the ✅ marks it used on the Lexicon index were removed 2026-10-29. Rulings (user) that generator scripts and new entries must follow; see also [[project-middle-semitic-lexicon-conventions]] and [[project-middle-semitic-paradigms-midtask]].

- **Copulas:** ܗܘ hô (past) and ܝܫ yiš (non-past) are EXISTENCE particles ('hô ôkâ' there was an owl; 'yiš êyalâ' there is a stag), no person/number. ܟܘܢ kôn 'to be equal' is a verb with a past only (no non-past). Present equality = 3rd-person pronoun alone when the subject is a pronoun ('hiyâ malkatâ' she is a queen) and a linker after a noun subject ('sarâ hiyâ malkatâ').
- **Adjectives:** four forms (m -â, f -tâ, pl -în, f pl -ût) plus frozen constructs (raqîq, raqîqî, raqîqat, raqîqû), lexical citation ends in long -â (rename bare stems); crypto-feminine nouns take feminine adjectives; colours are adjectives (ṣahabâ yellow, ṭalamâ black, waraqâ green, labânâ white); demonstratives hazâ/zakâ are full adjectives with NO construct; -ân adjectives (batûlânâ, rabbânâ, ḥaṭiyânâ); an adjective ending in a bare consonant is not an adjective (šukrân is an interjection). Script `AIOS/scripts/build_ms_adjectives.py` (--no-construct option). III-yod adjectives: first is ܥܒܝ 2 ʿabê 'thick' (Swadesh 30, homograph of ܥܒܝ 'forest') — ʿabê, ʿabêtâ, ʿabîn, ʿabêt, constructs on the samat/samû model; the f pl and construct forms are analogical, NOT yet ruled by the user.
- **Geminates (user, 2026-10-31; supersedes the 2026-10-28 simplify-before-consonant ruling):** geminate roots are NOT weak; they are conjugated as strong roots in every stem, nothing contracts (G barîr, D bîrrar, C šabrar, tD tibîrrar). Built only for the stative ܒܪܝܪ; no non-stative geminate verb exists yet, so the same is assumed for a geminate G. Roots: geminate roots now carry the `strong` tag (plus `geminate`), and the Roots.base 'Weak and guttural' view no longer lists them (a geminate with an aleph/mater/guttural radical is still weak by that radical).
- **III-yod nouns:** -ê in the singular, -în plural (gadê, gadîn, construct gadi, gadî); sky ܣܡܝ and year ܣܢܝ are crypto-feminine (sky: singular samê, dual samîm only in Genesis 1–6; year: rare dual).
- **Gender by majority** of Hebrew/Arabic/Aramaic cognates (Wiktionary) for the plain -a nouns; crypto-feminine when the spelling has no -t; ܥܫܬܪܬܐ is POS name, no chart.
- **Homographs:** ܣܠܘܡ (interjection hello) vs ܣܠܘܡܐ (masc. noun peace); ܨܒܥ 2 'he pointed' vs ܨܒܥ 'he painted'; ܒܪܐ 2 barâ 'he created (ex nihilo)', a theological loan verb, vs the noun ܒܪܐ barrâ; ܐܨܒܥ = noun 'finger' (like אצבע).
- **Torah page** (`Torah.md`): books as ## sections (Bereshit … Devarim), ### verse with Hebrew / Arabic cognate / Syriac Peshitta / MS table and translations. Done: Genesis 1:1 (pibadâtâ êlhâ barâ hal samîm wa-hal arṣâ) and Deuteronomy 6:4 (the Shema, removed from Dialogue). Syriac checked against peshitta.eu / ETCBC; the Arabic (Smith–Van Dyck) lines are from memory, unverified. ܦ 'in' is pi (pəMadrid); ܒ is 'at, on' only. ܒܕܐ badâ = 'he invented, devised, originated'; ܒܕܐܬܐ badâtâ = 'beginning'.
- **Stress:** closed final syllable takes it, else the penult; schwa never stressed; Latin rows of new entries show syllable dots and ˈ (see conventions memory).
- **Clitics (user, 2026-10-29):** six clitic prepositions: li- bi- pi- ki- and (new) mi- (ܡ, clitic of min) and ʿa- (ܥ, clitic of ʿal-); each has its own suffixed forms with a single consonant where the full word doubles (minî vs minnî, ʿanî vs ʿalnî), and each may reduce to a schwa in fast speech (mə-, ʿə-). Full words min, ʿal-, ʿim, bên keep stress. See `Prepositions.md`.
- **'Because' (user, 2026-10-29):** ܥܠܐܢ ʿal.ˈʾan = ʿal- + ʾan (complementizer), chosen over li- + ʾan (li- = goal, collides with kî 'in order that'). dal is only the genitive noun-linker; lîd the relative pronoun; ʾan the complementizer.
- **Dual of true feminines (user, 2026-10-31):** the dual is for body parts, glasses and pants in EVERY declension; a true-feminine body part builds it on the feminine stem with -îm (ܪܚܬܐ raḥtâ → raḥatîm, construct raḥatû, distinct from the plural construct raḥû), like Hebrew -atayim / Arabic -atān. ܪܚܬܐ is the only true-feminine body part so far.
- **Open:** brown = ܣܡܪܐ sa.ˈma.râ from S-M-R, and Ḥ-W-M means 'dark' (ܚܘܡܐ ˈḥô.mâ), both coined 2026-10-31 (red = ܚܡܪܐ ḥa.ˈma.râ from Ḥ-M-R, blue = ܙܪܩܐ za.ˈra.qâ from Z-R-Q, both coined 2026-10-31); retrofitting stress marks to existing Latin rows.
- **Stative, dual, construct (user, 2026-10-30):** statives have a long *î* after the first consonant. The dual is only for body parts and a few others (not just "glasses and pants"). The construct is kept because all ancient texts use it. Note: only *aw* → *ô* is an absolute no-sequence rule (see the conventions memory); *ay* is NOT banned as a sequence (no source language contracts it everywhere; e.g. ܡܝܐ *mayâ* 'water' stands).
- **Statives (user, 2026-10-30; supersedes the 2026-10-27 î-after-the-first-consonant ruling):** the stative vowel î is the SECOND vowel of the stem, historically correct and always identifiable: past 1a2î3 (ḥasîn), non-past ya1 2î3 (yaḥsîn), imperative ḥəsîn!, participle maḥsîn-, infinitive laḥsîn; no passive. It equals the adjective stem (ṣaʿîr / ṣaʿîrâ). The 11 stative verbs were renamed (ܚܝܣܢ → ܚܣܝܢ etc.; the geminate ܒܝܪ → ܒܪܝܪ barîr; III-waw ܚܝܠܘ → ܚܠܝܘ ḥalîw — the w stays a consonant after î, per the banyû rule; that verb was RETIRED on 2026-10-31 (user: we already have 'to be sweet', ܡܬܝܩ), so the III-waw stative rule is a stated rule with no model verb). Generator: `AIOS/scripts/build_ms_stative_verbs.py`; the `--stative` options of the other three verb generators now exit with a pointer to it.
- **Derived stems (user, 2026-10-30; not yet built):** labels G, Gp (the internal passive, 1û2a3), D, Dp, C, Cp, tD. Causative prefix is **š-** (old-school Proto-Semitic / Akkadian: distinctive, and no š- prefixes exist now). Reflexive tD prefix is **ti-** (spelled with just ܬ, a short i; a split between ʾit-/hit-/ta-; distinct from the ta- of the non-past). NO stem is assumed to exist for any verb: each lexical entry must say which of G, Gp, D, Dp, C, Cp, tD it has (true/false; the user suggested Obsidian checkbox properties). D is 1a22a3; the passives extend the û rule.
- **The passive û (user, 2026-10-30 clarification; matches the existing paradigm):** û always follows the FIRST CONSONANT OF THE FORM — the prefix in the non-past (yamlak → yûmlak; the prefix vowel becomes û), the first radical in the past (malak → mûlak). It does not wander to the first radical in the non-past. Consequences: Dp kûttab / non-past yûkattab; Cp past šûktab / non-past yûšaktab (û replaces the prefix vowel, the š- stays on the stem). In the participle and infinitive (ma-/la-) the second vowel changes (mamlûk-, lamlûk).
- **Stems table and properties (user, 2026-10-30):** each verb has true/false stem properties AND a Stems table in the body showing which stems it has and the altered meaning of each (ܟܬܒ: G to write, D to inscribe, C to dictate, tD to correspond — a plural-subject sense, 'they exchanged letters'). The tD template (first tikattab / yatikattab, then tikîttab / yatikîttab) (ti- on the D stem) is confirmed. Built for all 83 verbs (G/Gp from the pages), with ܟܬܒ as the pilot for D, C and tD; Dp and Cp are not checked for ܟܬܒ yet.
- **D stem has î first (user, 2026-10-30):** because the gemination mark is optional, the D (active) stem takes the vowel î after the first radical so it shows in the abjad alone, like Hebrew's piʿel: D = 1î22a3 (kîttab ܟܝܬܒ; non-past yakîttab); Dp = 1û22a3 (kûttab; non-past yûkîttab). The tD stem is ti- + the D stem: tikîttab ܬܟܝܬܒ, non-past yatikîttab (my consequence of the ruling, since tD was defined as the reflexive of D; this also removes the clash between tikattab and the G non-past taktab). Participle = ma- + the non-past stem, infinitive = la- + it, passives change the second vowel of the form to û (maktûb-, makûttab-, mašûktab-); imperative = the non-past stem alone (G with a schwa: kətab!), none for the passives. Only G/Gp drop the stem vowel before plural -û (katbû); the other stems cannot (an onset cluster would result). The full seven-stem mockup (14 tables, definitions list) is `Lexicon/ܟܬܒ.md`, built by `AIOS/scripts/build_ms_stem_paradigms.py`. KNOWN ISSUE (flagged to the user): Dp past kûttab is spelled like Gp past kûtab unless the shadda is written (Hebrew/Arabic have the same overlap); options if it must be fixed: a distinct Dp vowel pattern (e.g. Arabic-style kûttib), or an obligatory shadda in Dp.
- **tD, not T (user, 2026-10-30):** the reflexive stem is named tD so Arabic speakers read it as an analogue of stem V (not VI or VIII); property `stem-td`. Also: the Gp/Dp indistinguishability in unpointed text is accepted (the same overlap exists in Hebrew and Arabic); no change. A-K-L (ܐܟܠ, I-aleph) is the second seven-stem model: G to eat, D to devour/wolf, C to feed, tD to be eaten away/corroded; the root aleph fuses with a preceding a when a consonant follows (yâkal, šâkal) and stays a glottal stop after û, î, i (ʾûkal, ʾîkkal, tiʾîkkal).
- **M-L-K (user, 2026-10-30), the first verb that LACKS stems:** G to reign / rule over (x), C to enthrone (šamlak; Gp to be ruled over and Cp to be enthroned follow as passives); NO D, Dp or tD (properties false, rows marked ✗, no tables). `build_ms_stem_paradigms.py` records absent stems in its `ABSENT` dict. The old G/Gp Forms of ܡܠܟ were frozen into `AIOS/scripts/model_strong_forms.md`, which `build_ms_verbs.py` now reads (it used to read the ܡܠܟ page, which is now the seven-stem page). Open: ܡܠܟ's Part-of-speech row still says intransitive although G now means 'rule over x'; and Gp/Cp were assumed to exist (the user gave meanings only for G and C).
- **Diminutive (user, 2026-11-13):** masculine nouns add -ôna, feminine nouns add -ôta, after the Syriac diminutive -ōnā (brōnā "little son"); a diminutive declines by its own gender. First use: baḥrôtâ "lake" (the feminine diminutive of baḥrâ "sea", feminine because lake is feminine in the sources — Arabic buḥayra is likewise the diminutive of baḥr). Documented in Morphophonology (Nouns section); model page Lexicon/ܒܚܪܘܬܐ.md.
