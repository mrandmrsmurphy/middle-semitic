3. Exodus 20:2–17, the Ten Commandments, at least verses 3, 13, 14 and 15 ("you shall not murder, commit adultery, steal"). Musa's tablets are familiar, and the commandments are short.
6. Genesis 3:19, "dust you are, and to dust you shall return". It parallels Qur'an 20:55.
7. Leviticus 19:18, "love your neighbor as yourself". The Prophet's hadith "none of you believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself" runs close to it.
8. Exodus 3:14, ehyeh asher ehyeh, the name at the burning bush. Qur'an 20:11–14 has a parallel scene, "Indeed I am your Lord".

Two Torah verses that are less famous but Qur'an-adjacent:
- Genesis 2:7, man formed from dust. It's the Qur'anic ṭīn.
- Deuteronomy 6:5, "love the Lord your God with all your heart". It's the continuation of the Shema.

Prophets and Writings, if you want to go past the Torah:
- Psalm 23, "The Lord is my shepherd". It is the best known of all.
- Psalm 37:11, "the meek shall inherit the earth". Qur'an 21:105 quotes it, "My righteous servants will inherit the earth", and says it is written in the Zabūr.
- Isaiah 2:4, swords into plowshares.
- Ecclesiastes 3:1, "a time for everything". It's widely known and the Hebrew vocabulary is simple.
- Proverbs 1:7, "the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom". It parallels the Qur'anic taqwā.
- Psalm 1:1, the blessed man. It has an easy sentence structure.

My suggestion: do Genesis 1:3, Exodus 20:13–15 and Psalm 23 first.
- Exodus 20:13–15 are very short, and the commandments exercise the negative imperative (lo plus the verb).
- Psalm 23 is the most widely recognized text across all three faiths, and it tests the poetic register.
- For the vocabulary stress test: add Psalm 1:1 and Genesis 22:2 to see how many gaps MS has left.
